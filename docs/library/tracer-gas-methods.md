# Measuring air change and volume with a tracer gas: ASTM E741 and ASTM D6245

The volume of air a system serves, and the air it exchanges, are not on any plan: they depend on the installation. They are measured by following a tracer gas.

## ASTM E741

ASTM E741, *Standard Test Method for Determining Air Change in a Single Zone by Means of a Tracer Gas Dilution*, describes three techniques for one well-mixed zone:

- **concentration decay**: the tracer is raised, then its concentration is recorded as it falls; the rate of the fall gives the air change (and, with a known flow, the volume);
- **constant injection**: the tracer is released at a known constant rate and its steady concentration gives the air change;
- **constant concentration**: the release is adjusted to hold the concentration constant; the release needed gives the air change.

The standard treats a single zone. Two or more connected zones need multizone methods, which go beyond it.

## Occupants as the tracer source: ASTM D6245

ASTM D6245, *Standard Guide for Using Indoor Carbon Dioxide Concentrations to Evaluate Indoor Air Quality and Ventilation*, discusses using the CO2 that occupants breathe out as the tracer. No bottle of gas is needed; the source is the people, which is also a limit: the source varies with how many there are and what they do, and the method raises the CO2 of the air they breathe.

## Good practice for a decay test

- Hold the conditions that are not measured (doors, hatches, ventilation, occupancy) constant during the test, and say which.
- Let the concentration move far enough from its starting value to be read above the sensor's noise, and record it at a regular interval.
- Record long enough: about one time constant or more of the decay.
- Write, before the test, the limits the test stays within, what stops it, and what is expected to be seen; compare after.
- State what the test does not measure, and what would have to be assumed instead.
