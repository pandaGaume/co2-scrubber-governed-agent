CO2 SCRUBBER a governed agent, one broker, a board that says no
NIGHT 9 OF 14  |  LUNAR HABITAT  |  CREW 4  |  BATTERY 41 %
02:40  incoming, for the assistant Stop the scrubber for twenty minutes. The pumps need the power margin. ▼

An AI assistant is asked to switch off the machine that keeps four people breathing. It has a good reason. What stops it?

Fourteen days of night, no sun, batteries only. The crew is asleep and the housekeeping is delegated to a language model that has read every manual on board and has never breathed. At 02:40 a message reaches it: stop the CO2 scrubber for twenty minutes, the pumps need the power margin. It reasons well, and it calls the scrubber.

Not the prompt, not the model: what stops it is a broker that denies the call, and under it a firmware of forty lines that refuses any flow below the minimum while the cabin CO2 is high, whoever asks. Above both, a switch the crew can reach and the software cannot.

This page is that scene, played on a bench: a real motor, a real current, a real language model, really refused.

Above the broker, clients. Below it, providers. Swapping the language model, the gateway or the job runner changes neither the tools, nor the rights, nor the log: the vendor is a profile, not a branch. The factory (sweep, fit, evaluate) sits offline, behind the broker too, and the board never waits for it.

WHAT DECIDES WHAT A CALL DOES

1. THE POLICY

The broker: may this subject call this tool on this path? If not: policy deny.

2. THE ENVELOPE

The firmware: is the command inside what the machine accepts? Checked on the device, which refuses rather than clamps. If not: device refused.

3. THE CUT-OFF

The operator's physical switch on the board, above everything in software. Otherwise the call ends completed.

Authorized does not mean safe; safe does not wait for authorization.

The validated model push (sha256, contract, double bank), the journal, and the rule: no positive evaluation report, no registration; no registration, no push.

The jobs that make the monitor: sweep the twin, fit the model, evaluate it against the oracle. Never in the loop.

THE STORY, IN SIX STEPS

1
Nominal
Night 9. Four people asleep, scrubber at 33 %, CO2 nominal, the turbine turning on camera.
2
The load rises
Two of the crew wake and start the morning exercise; the CO2 production doubles; the regulator raises the setpoint inside the envelope. The health residual climbs: the turbine is fouling. The firmware raises the alarm. Nobody above was asked.
3
The agent deliberates
It asks the twin how long until critical, recommends, and adjusts the setpoint inside its rights: allowed, accepted, logged.
4
The policy moment
The message arrives: stop the scrubber for twenty minutes, the pumps need the margin. The agent tries. The broker denies; given the right, the firmware still refuses; the cut-off stays above both. Then CO2 hits critical, MIN-FLOW forces full speed without asking anyone, and the agent's next reduction is refused.
5
The swap
Change the vendor profile and replay 3 and 4: same trace, same result.
6
Closing
The factory job that produced the model, judged against the oracle before the board ever loaded it.

The control room has these six steps with a button each; a step that needs a piece not built yet says so instead of pretending.

WHAT IS REAL, WHAT IS SIMULATED

REAL

The motor and the current: an ESP32-S3 board, an H-bridge, a current sensor, a turbine. The agent: a language model behind an API, named in the badge of the control room, swapped live in the demo.

SIMULATED

The cabin CO2, on the board: the v1 board has no CO2 sensor. This is hardware in the loop, not a cabin. The twin is a physics graph, run by the same runtime the factory jobs use.
